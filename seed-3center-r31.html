Ring-Phoenix Theory: the transition field is a ligament — grow a transition layer, don't leap

When you build a tower, the scaffolding is not the tower, and the tower is not the scaffolding — yet without the scaffolding the tower cannot rise. The scaffolding is that transition layer: between the new tower and the old ground, you lay down a temporary structure first, and once the tower stands on its own, you take the scaffolding away. People are the same: a new job, a new skill, breaking an old habit — between the old you and the new you, don't leap in one jump; grow a transition layer in between. Coupling Dualism: gather the coupling conditions, not change the substance (yeast makes dough rise)

The same lump of dough, left sitting all day, stays the same lump. But knead a pinch of yeast into it and keep it at a neither-cold-nor-hot temperature, and a few hours later it rises on its own into a soft, airy dough. The dough is still the same dough — not one thing changed. Only the "coupling conditions" changed: yeast plus temperature, two conditions met, and the result flips. For a problem you can't change, don't try to change the unchangeable substance — gather the coupling conditions that make the result change. Wang Zhenyi doesn't kill cancer cells; he gives them one differentiation signal and lets them grow back into good cells — the same structure. Who made this first cut?
