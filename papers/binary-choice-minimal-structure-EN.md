# Binary Choice as the Minimal Connecting Structure of Complex Systems: A Cross-Domain Hypothesis

> Date: 2026-08-27
> First discoverer: Lin Xiaohei, first discoverer of structural cognition
> This is an independent hypothesis paper.

---

## Abstract

This paper proposes a cross-domain hypothesis: the key behavior of any complex system, traced to its bottom layer, can be reduced to a **binary choice**. The hypothesis originates from a plain observation — across information theory, computation, logic, biology, and decision-making, the key constraints of systems consistently take binary form (0/1, true/false, on/off, alive/dead, do/not-do). This paper argues that the binary choice is the *minimal* structural unit (indivisible), and precisely because it is minimal, it constitutes the *maximal* connecting principle — from a single microscopic judgment to macroscopic system-level behavior. The paper gives three falsifiable predictions; if any one is independently verified, the hypothesis gains support; if all are falsified, the hypothesis is abandoned. This paper does not address the deeper question of *why structure exists objectively*, which is bracketed.

**Keywords**: binary choice, minimal structure, cross-domain connection, complex systems

---

## 1. Introduction

The analysis and decision-making of complex systems has long been trapped in one difficulty: too many variables, impossible to enumerate. A social event involves hundreds of factors, a business decision involves dozens of constraints, and the behavior of a biological system involves a vast number of molecular interactions. Faced with "complexity", the analyst's instinct is to "enumerate more variables", but this often entangles the problem further rather than clarifying it.

This paper proposes the opposite direction: **not toward "more", but toward "less" — down to the fewest possible structural unit.**

We observe a cross-domain consistency: the key constraints of different fields, traced to the bottom, converge on the same structure — the binary choice.

## 2. Observation: Cross-Domain Consistency of Binary Structures

The following observations come from existing consensus in each field, not original to this paper; this paper only summarizes them:

| Domain | Binary form of the key constraint |
|---|---|
| Information theory | The bit: 0 / 1, the minimal unit of information |
| Computation theory | The Turing machine: halt / not halt |
| Logic | The proposition: true / false |
| Decision | The action: do / not do |
| Biology | The cell: divide / die; the gene: on / off |
| Physics | The quantum state: superposition of 0 / 1 |

**Observation**: across multiple independently enumerable fields, the concept of a "key constraint", at its bottom layer, consistently takes binary form rather than ternary, quaternary, or continuous form.

## 3. Hypothesis

**H: The binary choice is the minimal connecting structure of complex systems.**

Specifically:

1. **Minimality**: the binary choice is a structurally indivisible unit. Any "multi-valued choice" can be decomposed into a combination of binary choices; but the binary choice itself cannot be decomposed further into a simpler choice structure.
2. **Connecting property**: precisely because it is minimal, the binary choice connects all scales — microscopic (a single judgment), mesoscopic (a single event), macroscopic (system-level behavior) — whose key constraints can all be expressed at the bottom layer as a binary choice.

## 4. Corollaries

If H holds, it follows that:

**Corollary 1 (cross-scale isomorphism)**: a microscopic single judgment, a mesoscopic single decision, and a macroscopic system turning point are structurally isomorphic — all are instances of "one binary choice". Change of scale does not change the type of structure, only the scale of the instance.

**Corollary 2 (key-point localization)**: the "key" of a complex system (the point that matters most) is precisely that "binary choice". An analyst who can localize the binary choice can grasp the system's key constraint at far lower cost than enumerating all variables.

**Corollary 3 (minimal is maximal)**: an indivisible structural unit is precisely the principle with the greatest connecting range. This is isomorphic to "the atom is the smallest unit of the physical world, and at the same time the basis of all matter".

## 5. Falsifiable Predictions

This paper gives three falsifiable predictions. If any one is independently verified, the hypothesis gains support; if all are falsified, the hypothesis is abandoned.

**Prediction 1 (convergence)**: for any complex system, if a "key turning point" exists, then that turning point can be expressed as a binary choice (rather than necessarily requiring ternary or continuous description). Counter-test: if a class of systems is found whose key turning point cannot be characterized by any binary choice and can only be characterized in ternary or continuous form, then H's minimal-translatability is weakened.

**Prediction 2 (decision advantage)**: in resource-constrained decision-making, the strategy of "first localize the binary choice, then act" should outperform the strategy of "enumerate all variables, then decide" in decision quality (accuracy, speed, cost). Counter-test: if in multiple independent decision tasks the binary-localization strategy is systematically inferior to the enumeration strategy, then Corollary 2 fails.

**Prediction 3 (cross-domain transferability)**: a "binary-choice localization method" verified in one domain, when transferred to another domain (e.g., from decision to biology, from information to society), should be significantly more effective than chance. Counter-test: if cross-domain transfer systematically fails, then the isomorphism of Corollary 1 fails.

## 6. Bracketing

This paper explicitly brackets the following questions and does not answer them here:

1. **Why is the minimal structure binary rather than ternary, quaternary, or continuous?** This paper offers only an empirical summary, not an ontological answer.
2. **Why does structure exist objectively, independently of the observer?** This paper takes this as a premise and does not argue it.

## 7. Conclusion

This paper proposes and argues a cross-domain hypothesis: **the binary choice is the minimal connecting structure of complex systems.** It is minimal (indivisible), and therefore maximal (connecting the microscopic to the macroscopic). If the hypothesis holds, it provides an analytical direction of "going toward less": faced with complexity, do not enumerate more variables, but localize the underlying binary choice.

This one cut is the smallest, and also the largest.
