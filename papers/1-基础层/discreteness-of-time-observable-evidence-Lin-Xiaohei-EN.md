# The Discreteness of Time: Everyday Device Time Offsets as Observable Evidence

## —The offset is not error, but the macroscopic manifestation of discreteness

**Abstract**: We propose a falsifiable claim: the time offsets among everyday electronic devices (phones, watches, computers) are not "errors" to be corrected, but observable evidence for the discreteness of time. Mainstream understanding treats time as continuous, uniform, and identically flowing (Newtonian absolute time), with relativity (time dilation) significant only at high speed (special relativity) or strong gravity (general relativity). We argue that discreteness is observable at everyday scale: each device is an independent "body" that accumulates its own time via discrete clock-oscillation counts; the offset between two bodies is the difference of two discrete time projections. We give a falsifiable experimental design: measure the time evolution of multi-device offsets, and test whether they exhibit "stepwise jumps (discrete)" or "smooth drift (continuous error)". Observation of smooth random drift would falsify the claim.

**Keywords**: discreteness of time; coupled systems; device time offset; crystal oscillator; falsifiable experiment; relativity

## 1. Observational Starting Point

In everyday use, a phone and a watch almost never display identical time — the phone runs fast, the watch runs slow. This offset is not one-off but systematic and persistent.

## 2. Core Claim

**The offset is not "error"; it is determined by the discreteness of a coupled system.**

- Each electronic device's time is, in essence, the accumulated count of a crystal oscillator (quartz oscillator). Each oscillation increments a counter by one; so-called "time" is the macroscopic representation of this discrete count.
- Counting is discrete: 1, 2, 3… there is no 1.5 oscillations. The "time" of two devices is the accumulated result of two separate discrete counts.
- Hence the offset between two devices is the difference of two discrete count sequences — it is discrete and stepwise, not continuous and smooth.

Mainstream understanding explains "device time offset" as "accumulated crystal frequency error" (ppm-level error), treating it as a defect to be corrected. This paper argues: error is the appearance, discreteness is the essence. Even if the crystal were made perfectly error-free, the time of two independent devices could never be fully identical — because each device's time is an independent discrete count, and there is no external "continuous absolute time" against which they can align.

## 3. The Accepted Belief Being Challenged

The accepted belief: time is continuous, uniform, and identically flowing (Newtonian absolute time); relativity is significant only under extreme conditions (special relativity: near light speed; general relativity: strong gravitational field).

Our claim: discreteness/relativity of time is observable at everyday scale. The offset between two devices is direct evidence that "time is not continuous and not identical". No near-light-speed motion, no black hole required — the watch on the wrist and the phone in the pocket demonstrate this fact every day, but it has been read as "error" instead of "structure".

## 4. Falsifiable Experimental Design

**Experiment**: Take a set of devices (phones, watches, computers, tablets, multiple samples of each), record the displayed time of each at the same moment, sample repeatedly (e.g., every 30 seconds over 24 hours), and record the time evolution of the offset of each device pair.

**Prediction**: If time is discrete, the offset between devices should evolve in stepwise jumps (discrete, discontinuous jumps) rather than smooth drift. The minimal "step" (minimal jump magnitude) of the offset should be related to the clock reference period of the device (the order of the reciprocal of the crystal frequency).

**Falsification condition**: If the observed offset is smooth, continuous random drift (white noise) that cannot be decomposed into discrete steps, the "discreteness of time" claim is falsified.

## 5. Falsifiable Predictions

1. **Step prediction**: The offset of the same device pair (phone + watch), over a 24-hour observation window, changes in discrete stepwise jumps rather than a continuous ramp. There exists a non-zero minimal unit of jump magnitude.
2. **Lattice prediction**: The statistical distribution of relative offsets across multiple devices shows discrete lattice points (quantized discrete values), not a continuous uniform distribution.
3. **Quantum-frequency correlation prediction**: The minimal jump unit of a device's offset is of the same order as the reciprocal of that device's clock reference frequency (crystal frequency). That is, the "quantum" of discreteness is a function of the device's clock period.

All three are falsifiable predictions — negation of any one constitutes a refutation of the claim.

## 6. Bracketing

This paper does not assert the heavier physical claim that "time is quantized at the Planck time", nor does it assert any replacement relation to existing theories of time. It asserts only a testable, weaker claim: the discreteness of everyday device time offsets is observable, and its "discrete step" is testable at the order of the device clock period. Whether this discreteness extends downward to more fundamental physical scales is bracketed and left unasserted.

## 7. Conclusion

Time is discrete, not continuous. The offset between everyday devices is observable evidence of this discreteness, not error. This claim can be tested with a set of devices, 24 hours of sampling, and three falsifiable predictions — it depends on no extreme physical conditions, only on the clocks everyone carries.
