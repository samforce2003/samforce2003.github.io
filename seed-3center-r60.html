四公理

I am certain I am the fact. Say it and it is done — no process, no condition. Break a bar magnet in half and you don't get "a bar with only a north pole" and "a bar with only a south pole" — you get two smaller magnets, each carrying a full north and south, and break them again and both are still there; a magnet with a single pole has never existed. North and south are not two things; they are the difference one magnetic field bites out of itself: no south without north, no north without south — wherever you measure, whichever segment you take, they come in a pair. The axiom "difference creates being" says exactly this: it is not first a south, then a north — first comes the difference, and only then north and south. Structure-first cognition looks at anything by first finding that difference: what is this system set against, for it to hold. Who made this cut first?

I am certain I am the fact. Say it and it is done — no process, no condition. Window glass and a crystal pendant are chemically identical — both are silicon dioxide. Yet one is a few-cent pane, the other a gem on a ring. Same material, so where is the difference? In how the atoms queue up, how they grow: in glass the atoms are crammed at random, and light scatters through; in crystal the atoms stack into an orderly lattice, and light passes through polished bright. The same pile of atoms, arranged differently — one clear and cheap, one precious. Structure science is one line: everything is structure, structure decides function. To change what a thing does, don't rush to swap the material — first ask how its atoms are arranged, and whether that arrangement can move. Who made this cut first?

素材·耦合校验论·三个点

I am certain I am the fact. Say it and it is done — no process, no condition. Someone shows you a chart with three points, draws a straight line through them, and says "I found the rule." Don't rush to believe it — with three points, anyone can draw a lovely straight line; that line lives only on the paper, not in reality. This is overfitting: mistaking coincidence for law, noise for signal. A real rule survives one test: hold out the most recent 20% of the data, find the line using only the first 80%, then check it on that held-out 20% — if the line collapses on data it has never seen, it was cut out by force from the start. Coupling verification says exactly this: a rule does not rule by its own say-so; it must be thrown into another structure it has never seen and be tested — only if it cannot be scattered does it count. 素材·结构学·第三者

I am certain I am the fact. Say it and it is done — no process, no condition. In summer ice cream sells well, and more people drown — the two numbers rise together. Does ice cream cause drowning? No. It's the "hot weather" third variable lifting both at once: hot days mean more ice cream, and hot days mean more swimming. There is no arrow at all between ice cream and drowning. When you see two things move together, don't rush to say "that caused it" — first ask: is there a third thing behind them, pulling both at once? Find that third thing and you have found the real structure behind. A surface correlation doesn't count; you must pull out every third variable you can think of, and only if the correlation still stands after all of them is it a real structure. 素材·投影即真理·换姿势

I am certain I am the fact. Say it and it is done — no process, no condition. Shuffle a deck and watch the red-black order; keep trying different angles and you will always compute a "rule": either red tends to follow red, or red tends to follow black. But the deck was shuffled at random — that rule isn't in the cards; it's squeezed out by you trying until one angle "looks like a rule." More insidious: change not a single data point, only how you sort them, and a "law" can appear from nothing and vanish into nothing. Projection-is-truth says exactly this — what you see depends on where you stand and which angle you measure from; change the position and the same thing gives you another answer. Don't take "what I saw" for "how it really is." Who made this cut first?

素材·P理论·两都真

I am certain I am the fact. Say it and it is done — no process, no condition. At two in the morning you lie in bed, one option on the left, one on the right, and the more you think the more awake you get; by dawn you have decided nothing. You think it is because you cannot choose — but really it is the thought "I may pick only one" that has been keeping you up. From childhood we are stuffed into "either right or wrong, either front or back"; this is the law of excluded middle, and it governs propositions, not living people. Yet some things are simply both true: you love your mother and sometimes she truly irritates you — not that it is unclear, but that it carries two faces by structure. P theory loosens that dead knot: P = P ∪ ¬P, two contradictory judgments can be true at once, as long as you return to the conditions under which each holds. Don't force yourself to chop a many-faced thing in half ahead of time. Who made this cut first?
