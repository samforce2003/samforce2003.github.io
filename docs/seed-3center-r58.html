Ethanol and dimethyl ether have the exact same formula, C₂H₆O — two carbons, six hydrogens, one oxygen, not one atom more or less. Yet ethanol is wine: a person drinks it and gets drunk. Dimethyl ether is poison: a person drinks it and dies. The same six atoms — only 'how they are linked' differs: which atom the oxygen hangs on, which chain they form. That single difference in arrangement splits 'drinkable' from 'deadly'. This is the first cut of structure theory: all is structure, structure decides function. Not one atom of material changed — only the arrangement — and the function flips completely. Look at people the same way: same resources, same time, arranged differently, and the outcome is two different things. Who made this cut first?

One phenomenon, computed at the state level, gives a positive correlation of +0.53 — states with more immigrants have higher literacy. Computed at the individual level, it flips to −0.11 — immigrant individuals are actually more likely to be illiterate. Neither number is wrong; what is wrong is using a group-level pattern to answer an individual question. Aggregation erases within-group differences and lets between-group differences jump out to dominate the result. Whatever level you compute at, that is the 'truth' you see — there is no absolutely objective number; the observing position decides what you see. If you want to conclude about a level, compute at that level. Who made this cut first?
