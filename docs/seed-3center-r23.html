You don't understand first and then use it; you use it until you truly understand, and once you do, it grows itself into your method. Most people who are stuck aren't lazy — they keep grinding one plane they never cut right. Cut the structure first, talk effort later.

Faucet Critical Point: the click when change flips over

Tuning a shower, you turn the handle a hair at a time, and the hot side suddenly goes 'click' — it was cold the whole way, and in one instant the water turns hot. That click is the Faucet Critical Point. Cold water is homogeneous, which means silence; hot water rushing in is the signal. It teaches one thing: stop chasing the data curve. The smooth part of the curve carries no information — what matters is the click, the instant something flips from 'unchanged' to 'changed'. A real change usually doesn't arrive gradually; it flips over at some critical point, click. Your job isn't to stare at the curve every day; it's to keep your ears up and wait for that one sound.
