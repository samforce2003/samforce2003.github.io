Coupling Dualism: one meta-dual template, coupling and decoupling give rise to everything

Day and night can't exist without each other. No night, and you'd never know what 'day' means; no day, and the word 'night' loses its meaning. The hardest law in the universe isn't some single 'thing' — it's pairs of duality: the A-end and the not-A-end. Remove one, and the other vanishes on the spot. Coupling Dualism says the universe has one meta-dual template, and everything is this template coupling and decoupling over and over. Three axioms: ① Duality — the A-end and the not-A-end, neither survives alone; ② Universal isomorphism — the underlying code is identical everywhere, only the surface differs in ten thousand ways; ③ Possibility and constraint — superposition and the force of collapse coexist forever. The myriad world you see isn't countless different things; it's one template wearing ten thousand coats.

Coupling Control Theory: collapse-state and superposition-state coexist — hold both

Ask someone 'do you want to quit your job?' and they say 'yes, and no.' That isn't evasion — it's the most honest thing they can say. Coupling Control Theory says the collapse-state and the superposition-state coexist by nature. Classical logic forces you to choose one — quit or stay, either-or. But a real person sits in both states at once: 'want to quit' and 'don't want to quit,' both true, both present. Control theory doesn't ask you to pick one; it asks you to hold both — allow opposition, contain two directions. The mark of a mature system isn't which side it chose, but whether it can hold two truths at once. The collapse-state theorem (things must eventually land on one result) and the superposition corollary (before they land, every possibility is alive) both hold; neither eats the other. This isn't contradiction — it's both-and. The world isn't a single-choice question; it's a superposition. Treat it as single-choice and you tear yourself apart.

Coupling Signal Theory: consciousness = signal + structural coupling

The same single 'mm' in a text: from your mom it means 'I heard you'; from your boss it means 'understood, go handle it'; from someone you're flirting with, you read three hundred meanings into it at midnight. Same character, same signal — why so different in meaning? Coupling Signal Theory says: because the signal itself is neutral; it's structural coupling that gives it meaning. A signal lands on some structure, and that structure decodes it accordingly. Landed on the mother-child structure, it becomes reassurance; on the boss-subordinate structure, a command; on the undefined structure of a crush, a thousand possibilities. Consciousness is the same — consciousness isn't the signal itself, it's the moment the signal crashes into structure. The 'meaning' you see was never inside the signal; it's in that one collision between signal and structure.
